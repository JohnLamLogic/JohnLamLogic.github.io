Research

ARIS — Authenticated Randomness Infrastructure Service

Senior Design · University of Miami NanoMagnetoElectronics Lab · Completed May 2026

ARIS is a hardware-based True Random Number Generator (TRNG) that derives entropy from Magneto-Optical Kerr Effect (MOKE) hysteresis measurements of magnetic thin-film samples. The system integrates a Stanford Research SR865A dual-phase lock-in amplifier with FPGA processing to produce cryptographically auditable random bitstreams.

  • Designed and automated a MOKE measurement system using LabVIEW, SR865A lock-in amplifier at 255 Hz chopper reference, and free-space optical alignment — improving sample throughput ~6× over prior manual workflow
  • Debugged AC/DC overload conditions in the lock-in amplifier chain caused by stray reflections in the optical path
  • Architected FPGA pipeline to sample lock-in output, apply NIST SP 800-22 statistical validation, and stream entropy to host
  • Von Neumann debiasing, SHA-256 expansion into 256-bit blocks, and Ed25519 signing; every accepted block is appended to a hash-chained audit log — a tamper-evident chain of 11,151 blocks
  • All 15 NIST SP 800-22 tests pass on the combined 2.8-million-bit dataset; an independent verifier confirms signature validity, chain integrity, and timestamp precedence from the public key alone
  • REST API serves entropy to three demonstrations: an authenticated blackjack game, a Monte Carlo portfolio risk simulation, and a fair audit-sampling tool
  • Presented as “Randomness as a Service” at the University of Miami Senior Design Expo 2026
